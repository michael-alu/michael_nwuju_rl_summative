    How the game works

    The job. An agent looks after a ₦10 million pension fund for 60 days. It must pay every retiree on time. Some retirements are known 5 to 15 days ahead; others, like early withdrawals and death benefits, give only 2 or 3 days' notice.

    What the fund owns. Cash, which pays retirees. Government bills, which sell instantly. Bank shares, which sell easily but the money arrives the next day. Small-company shares, which are hard to sell - few people trade them, and selling a lot pushes the price down.

    One move a day. Wait, sell some government bills, sell some bank shares, sell some small-company shares, or call a big buyer who takes a large chunk of small-company shares at once, but only on days one is around.

    Two rules. Small-company shares can be at most 35% of the fund (the red ring), and bank shares must stay between 35% and 70% (the green box). Paying retirees shrinks the fund, so these shares slowly grow as a share of it. A broken rule must be fixed within 10 days.

    Forced sales. If there is not enough cash on a payment day, or a broken rule is not fixed in time, the game sells for the agent at a bad price. One forced sale loses the game.

    Why it is hard. Small-company shares rarely trade, so their price on the exchange often stays the same for days while their real value moves. The agent only sees that price. The chart at the bottom shows both.

    The selling budget. Cash runs out, so the fund must sell things to pay retirees, and every sale costs a little - exchange fees, and the price dips when you sell a lot. So the fund gets a selling budget of ₦125,000 (1.25% of the fund), and each sale uses some of it. The office scoreboard shows how much is left.

    How to win. Pay every retiree on time, never be forced to sell, and finish with budget left.
